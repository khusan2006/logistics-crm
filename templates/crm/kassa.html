Six tiles, each one place money or goods is sitting right now, each with the second fact that makes it actionable — kg, mijoz count, yuk count — and each linking to the screen where you act on it. Nothing is summed across them on purpose: adding cash to granula to somebody else's unpaid invoice gives a number that describes no actual thing. A tile reads one of two ways, and the difference is deliberate. A tile with a `split` prints one full-size figure PER CURRENCY, because that is how many facts it holds: the till keeps a dollar heap and a so'm heap, and a hamkor owed on a dollar kelishuv and a so'm one is owed two separate debts. Neither side is the subordinate of the other, so neither is set in the smaller type. A tile without one keeps the older stacked pair — the dollar figure with its so'm twin beneath — because its figure is a COST, and a cost blends on purpose: a kg has one landed price even though the mol was bought in dollars and the transport paid in so'm. Omborda is that, and so is a logist's hisob, which is kept in dollars. {% endcomment %}
